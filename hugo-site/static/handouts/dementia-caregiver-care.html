Caregiver Stress & Self-Care

You can't pour from an empty cup. Caring for someone with dementia is a marathon, and looking after yourself isn't selfish — it's what makes the caregiving sustainable.

⚠

Signs of Burnout

  • Exhaustion that rest doesn't fix
  • Irritability, resentment, or crying easily
  • Pulling away from friends and things you enjoy
  • Trouble sleeping; getting sick often
  • Feeling hopeless or that you're never doing enough
💕

Hard Emotions Are Normal

Grief, guilt, anger, and loss are part of this — often all at once. You may grieve the person while they're still here (ambiguous loss).

Feeling frustrated or wishing for relief doesn't make you a bad caregiver. It makes you human.

🌱

Protect Your Basics

  • Guard your sleep — trade night duty when you can.
  • Eat real meals; move your body daily, even briefly.
  • Keep your own medical appointments.
  • Step outside for daylight and a few deep breaths.
🤝

Accept & Ask for Help

  • Let others help — give specific tasks (a meal, a ride, an hour).
  • Use respite care: adult day programs, in-home aides, short stays.
  • You don't have to do this alone, and you're not meant to.
  • Build a small team before you're in crisis.
👥

Find Support

  • Join a caregiver support group — in person or online.
  • Alzheimer's Association Helpline: 1-800-272-3900 (24/7).
  • Ask about counseling — caregiver depression is common and treatable.
  • Talk to people who understand; it lightens the load.
