Planning Ahead: Legal, Financial & Care

Hard as it is to face early, planning while your loved one can still take part is a gift to everyone. It protects their wishes and spares you agonizing decisions later.

⏰

Start Early

Legal documents require the person to have capacity — the ability to understand the decision. In early dementia that's often still possible, so don't wait.

Involve them in their own wishes while you can. It honors their voice.

📜

Key Legal Documents

  • Durable power of attorney — names who manages finances.
  • Healthcare proxy / POA — names who makes medical decisions.
  • Advance directive / living will — their wishes for care.
  • Will and updated beneficiaries.
💰

Financial Steps

  • Gather accounts, bills, insurance, and passwords in one place.
  • Set up bill autopay to prevent missed payments.
  • Watch for scams — people with dementia are targeted.
  • Meet with an elder-law attorney or financial planner.
🏠

Care Options & Costs

  • In-home care — aides for part- or full-day help.
  • Adult day programs — supervised daytime activity.
  • Assisted living / memory care — residential support.
  • Ask what Medicare, Medicaid, or long-term-care insurance cover — it varies.
🤝

Build a Care Team

  • Primary clinician plus a specialist (neurology, geriatrics, psychiatry).
  • Care manager or social worker to coordinate.
  • Family meeting to share roles and decisions.
  • If no one can safely decide and no POA exists, ask about guardianship.
