Behavioral Activation & Activity Scheduling

Depression tells you to wait until you feel like doing things. Behavioral activation flips that: action comes first, and the motivation follows.

🔴

The Depression Trap

When we feel low, we do less. Doing less means fewer good moments, which makes us feel lower — a downward spiral.

The way out is to act your way into feeling better, adding small activities before the motivation arrives. Motivation follows action, not the other way around.

⚡

Two Kinds of Activities

  • Pleasure: things that feel good — music, a walk, a favorite show, time with a pet.
  • Mastery: things that give a sense of accomplishment — a chore, a bill, a small task.

Aim for a mix of both each day.

🧭

Start Tiny

  • Break it down: not "clean the house" but "clear one counter."
  • Schedule it — a time and day, not "sometime."
  • Do it even if you don't feel like it. That's the whole point.
  • Notice mood before and after; the lift is your evidence.

Ideas to Draw From

  • Step outside for 10 minutes of daylight
  • Text or call one person
  • Cook or eat one real meal
  • Tidy one small space
  • Do a hobby you used to enjoy, even briefly
  • Move — stretch, walk, dance to one song
🌱

Be Kind About Setbacks

Some days you'll do less. That's not failure — it's depression, and it's exactly why we schedule instead of wait.

Missed yesterday? Pick one small thing today. Progress is measured over weeks, not days.

Track it for a week. Rate your mood 0–10 before and after each activity. Most people discover that even small actions nudge mood upward — and seeing that pattern makes the next step easier.
