Handouts · Medications

Antihistamines & Anticholinergics

Includes diphenhydramine (Benadryl), hydroxyzine (Vistaril), and benztropine (Cogentin). Used for anxiety, sleep, itching, or to ease medication-related muscle stiffness.

💊

What These Are

These medicines block histamine and/or acetylcholine in the body and brain.

• Hydroxyzine (Vistaril) and diphenhydramine (Benadryl) are antihistamines used for anxiety, sleep, or itching.
• Benztropine (Cogentin) is an anticholinergic used to ease muscle stiffness or tremor caused by antipsychotic medicines.

🍽

How to Take It

Take with or without food. The antihistamines are sedating — if used for sleep or anxiety, they are often taken at night or as needed.

Benztropine is usually taken on a set schedule. Do not exceed the prescribed dose.

🛑

Important Cautions

There is no “black box” warning, but a few cautions are important:

• Best avoided as a regular medicine in older adults — they raise the risk of confusion, falls, and memory problems.
• Hydroxyzine can affect heart rhythm (QT) at higher doses — tell us about any heart-rhythm history.
• Diphenhydramine is dangerous in overdose.

🩹

Common (Usually Mild) Side Effects

  • Drowsiness.
  • Dry mouth, dry eyes.
  • Blurred vision.
  • Constipation, trouble urinating.
⚠

Serious — Call Us or Seek Care

  • Confusion, agitation, or hallucinations — especially in older adults.
  • Cannot urinate, or severe eye pain/blurred vision (can worsen certain glaucoma).
  • Fast or irregular heartbeat, or fainting.
  • Overheating — these reduce sweating, so be careful in hot weather or hard exercise.
🚫

Alcohol & Interactions

Avoid alcohol — it adds strongly to the drowsiness.

Tell us about other sedatives and about any other anticholinergic medicines — the effects stack up and raise the risk of confusion.

🧪

Monitoring

No routine blood tests are needed.

In older adults, we keep an eye on thinking, balance, and fall risk, and try to limit long-term use. An EKG may be checked before higher-dose hydroxyzine if you have heart-rhythm risks.

This is general information, not a substitute for your prescriber's advice. Never start, stop, or change a dose on your own — some of these medicines need a slow, supervised taper. Bring this sheet to your appointments and ask questions.
