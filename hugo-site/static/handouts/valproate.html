Valproate / Divalproex (Depakote)

A mood stabilizer for bipolar disorder (and seizures and migraine). Effective, but it carries important warnings, especially in pregnancy.

💊

What It Is

Valproate steadies mood and is especially useful for manic or mixed episodes in bipolar disorder. It also treats seizures and prevents migraines.

🍽

How to Take It

Take with food to reduce stomach upset. Swallow extended-release tablets whole.

Take at the same times each day; do not stop suddenly.

🛑

Black Box Warnings

  • Liver injury — rare but serious; highest risk in young children and early on.
  • Pancreatitis — can occur at any time; severe belly pain, nausea, vomiting.
  • Birth defects and lower IQ if taken in pregnancy — avoid if you could become pregnant unless there is no alternative and reliable contraception is used.
🩹

Common (Usually Mild) Side Effects

  • Nausea, upset stomach.
  • Hand tremor.
  • Weight gain, drowsiness.
  • Hair thinning (a zinc/selenium supplement may help).
⚠

Serious — Seek Care Now

  • Liver problems: yellow skin/eyes, dark urine, unusual tiredness, loss of appetite.
  • Pancreatitis: severe upper-belly pain with vomiting.
  • Easy bruising or bleeding (low platelets).
  • Confusion or extreme sleepiness (high ammonia).
🚫

Alcohol & Interactions

Avoid alcohol — it adds stress to the liver and to sedation.

Valproate raises lamotrigine levels and interacts with several other medicines — keep your list current.

🧪

Required Monitoring

• Liver tests (LFTs) — at baseline and periodically
• Blood count and platelets (CBC)
• Valproate blood level to guide dosing
• Ammonia if you become confused or very drowsy
Tell us right away if you might be pregnant.

This is general information, not a substitute for your prescriber's advice. Never start, stop, or change a dose on your own — some of these medicines need a slow, supervised taper. Bring this sheet to your appointments and ask questions.
