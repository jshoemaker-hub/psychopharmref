Getting Help & Resources

Where to turn for support with domestic or intimate partner violence. Everything here is free and confidential. You do not have to have made any decision to reach out.

🚨

If You're in Danger Now

Call 911 if you or your children are in immediate danger.

To quickly leave a website, close the tab or press the Esc key. Internet and phone use can be tracked — consider using a device the other person can't access (a friend's phone, or a computer at a library).

📞

  • National Domestic Violence Hotline
    Call 1-800-799-7233 (1-800-799-SAFE)
    Text START to 88788 · chat at thehotline.org
  • Deaf / hard of hearing: video phone 1-855-812-1001
  • StrongHearts Native Helpline: 1-844-762-8483
  • love is respect (teens & young adults): 1-866-331-9474, text LOVEIS to 22522
  • 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline: call or text 988

Interpreters are available in many languages. You can call just to talk through your options.

🤝

What an Advocate Can Do

A domestic violence advocate is a trained, confidential supporter — not the police and not the court. They can:

  • Listen without judgment and help you sort out your options
  • Help you make a safety plan
  • Connect you to emergency shelter, housing, and financial help
  • Explain protective orders and go with you to court
  • Help with children, benefits, and immigration questions
📱

Staying Safer Online

  • Use a device the other person can't access when looking for help.
  • Check your phone for tracking or "find my" apps and shared locations.
  • Change passwords from a safe device; turn off location sharing.
  • Clearing browser history can help — but a sudden empty history can raise suspicion; a separate safe device is safer.
  • Be cautious with the patient portal and shared email — ask your clinician what's safe to send home.
🎒

Planning Ahead

If you ever need to leave quickly, it helps to prepare early. Consider keeping (somewhere safe or with someone you trust):

  • IDs, birth certificates, immigration and insurance papers
  • Some cash and a card; a spare set of keys
  • Medications and a list of them; important phone numbers
  • A few things for the children and any comfort items
  • A code word with a trusted person that means "call for help"

An advocate can help you build a full plan. See also our Safety Plan worksheet.

⚖

Legal Protections

  • Protective / restraining orders can legally order the other person to stay away. Advocates and legal aid can help you apply.
  • Keep a record — dates, photos of injuries, threatening messages — stored somewhere safe.
  • Immigrants: protections like a VAWA self-petition or a U visa may let you seek status without the other person. Ask for an immigration attorney or a DV legal-aid program.
  • Legal help is often free through domestic violence programs.
