Setting Healthy Boundaries

A boundary is where you end and someone else begins. It's not a wall to keep people out — it's a line that lets relationships stay respectful and lets you stay well.

🧭

What a Boundary Is

A boundary is a clear statement of what's okay and not okay with you — your time, body, energy, and values. It names your limit and what you will do, not an attempt to control the other person.

Healthy boundaries actually build closeness: people know where they stand with you.

🚩

Signs You Need One

  • Resentment, dread, or exhaustion around a person
  • Saying yes when you mean no
  • Feeling responsible for others' feelings
  • Over-explaining or apologizing constantly
💬

How to Say It

Clear, kind, and brief. A simple formula:

"When ___ happens, I feel ___. Going forward, I need ___."

Example: "When plans change last minute, I feel stressed. Going forward, I need a day's notice."

✅

Make It Stick

  • State it once, calmly — you don't owe a debate.
  • "No" is a complete sentence. No essay required.
  • Name the follow-through: what you'll do if it's crossed.
  • Then actually follow through — that's what makes it real.
🛡

Guilt Is Normal

If you're used to putting others first, boundaries feel selfish at first. They aren't. Guilt is just the discomfort of a new habit — not proof you did wrong.

People who benefited from you having no limits may push back. That's information, not a reason to fold.
