Cannabis Use

Cannabis is legal in many places, but "legal" doesn't mean risk-free. For some people daily use quietly takes a toll on mood, motivation, and sleep. Here's how to think it through.

🟩

Is My Use a Problem?

It may be worth a change if you:

  • Use more, or more often, than you meant to.
  • Need more to get the same effect.
  • Have tried to cut down and couldn't.
  • Notice it affecting work, school, money, or relationships.
🧠

What Regular Use Can Do

  • Dull memory, focus, and motivation, especially with daily use.
  • Worsen anxiety, and raise psychosis risk in vulnerable people.
  • Disrupt sleep quality over time, even if it helps you fall asleep.
  • Today's products are far stronger than years past.
🌬

Withdrawal Is Real

Stopping after regular use can bring irritability, poor sleep, vivid dreams, low appetite, restlessness, and cravings.

It usually starts in a day or two, peaks in the first week, and fades within about two weeks. Knowing it's temporary makes it easier to ride out.

💪

Cutting Back

  • Set a clear goal — less often, lower amount, or a full break.
  • Taper gradually rather than all at once if daily use is heavy.
  • Delay the first use of the day; push it later each day.
  • Replace the routine (walk, shower, call someone) with a new habit.
⚠

Higher-Risk Situations

  • Pregnancy and breastfeeding.
  • Teens and young adults — the developing brain is more sensitive.
  • A personal or family history of psychosis.
  • Driving — cannabis impairs reaction time.
🤝

Getting Support

There's no FDA-approved medication yet, but counseling and skills-based support work well.

  • Ask your clinician about therapy for cannabis use.
  • SAMHSA National Helpline: 1-800-662-4357 (free, confidential, 24/7).
Cyclic vomiting? Repeated bouts of severe nausea and vomiting that hot showers briefly relieve can be cannabinoid hyperemesis syndrome. The only reliable fix is to stop using — tell your clinician.
