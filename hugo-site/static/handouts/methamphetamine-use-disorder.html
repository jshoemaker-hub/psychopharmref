Methamphetamine Use Disorder

Meth is powerful and the pull can feel overwhelming, but recovery is real. Behavioral treatments work, and knowing what to expect makes the early weeks easier to survive.

⚡

What Meth Does

Methamphetamine floods the brain with dopamine, driving intense energy and euphoria — followed by a hard crash.

Use often falls into binges, then exhaustion. Over time the brain's reward system needs the drug just to feel normal, which is what makes stopping so hard.

❤

Health Effects

  • Sleeplessness, weight loss, and dental damage.
  • High blood pressure, chest pain, stroke, and heart strain.
  • Anxiety, paranoia, and sometimes psychosis.
  • Skin sores from picking; risky decisions when high.
🏆

What Treatment Works

There's no FDA-approved medication yet, but behavioral treatments are effective:

  • Contingency management — rewards for drug-free tests; the strongest evidence.
  • Cognitive behavioral therapy and the Matrix Model.
  • Community and peer support to rebuild routines.
😴

The Crash & Early Recovery

Stopping brings deep fatigue, heavy sleep, low mood, big appetite, and strong cravings. This is the brain healing, not a sign you can't do it.

The fog lifts over days to weeks. Sleep, food, and patience are the medicine here.

🛡

Staying Safer

  • Don't use alone; the supply may be mixed with fentanyl.
  • Keep naloxone nearby in case of opioid contamination.
  • Hydrate, and don't mix with other stimulants or alcohol.
  • Seek care for chest pain, severe agitation, or paranoia.
🤝

Getting Support

  • Ask your clinician about programs that offer contingency management.
  • SAMHSA National Helpline: 1-800-662-4357 (free, confidential, 24/7).
  • Recovery is rarely a straight line — keep going after a setback.
Rewards beat willpower. Contingency management — small, concrete rewards for negative drug tests — has the strongest evidence for stimulant use. Ask what programs are available near you.
