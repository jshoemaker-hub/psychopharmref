SMART Goals & Committed Action

Big change happens through small, specific steps taken again and again. SMART goals make the step clear; committed action means doing it even when motivation dips.

🎯

Start With What Matters

A goal sticks when it's tied to a value — who you want to be, not just what you want to check off. "Be a present parent" or "care for my health" gives goals meaning and staying power.

✅

Make It SMART

  • Specific — exactly what will you do?
  • Measurable — how will you know it's done?
  • Achievable — realistic for you right now?
  • Relevant — tied to your value?
  • Time-bound — by when?
🔠

Vague vs. SMART

Vague: "Exercise more."

SMART: "Walk 20 minutes after lunch on Mon/Wed/Fri this week."

The second one you can actually picture doing — and know whether you did.

🧶

Committed Action

  • Motivation comes and goes; commitment is a choice you renew.
  • Shrink the step until it feels almost too easy to skip.
  • Expect obstacles — plan for them in advance.
  • Fell off? Recommit to the next step. One slip isn't failure.
🛠

Beat the Roadblocks

  • If (obstacle), then (my plan). Fill both in ahead of time.
  • Tie the step to a cue you already have (after coffee, before bed).
  • Tell someone — shared goals get done more often.
📝

My SMART Goal

This week I will:

By when:

If ___ gets in the way, then I'll:

Set the bar low enough to clear on a bad day. A goal you hit builds momentum; one you miss drains it. You can always do more — but start with the version you're confident you'll actually do.
