Psychiatric pharmacology is usually taught as a list of receptors. That works until a patient is febrile, acidotic, hyponatraemic, or seizing — at which point you need to know which cell the receptor sits on, what that cell's metabolic reserve is, and which systemic variable it cannot tolerate. This section is organised by cell type for that reason. Eighteen populations, each with its anatomy, normal physiology, receptor and transporter complement, the diseases that are genuinely well understood, and the systemic perturbations that alter its function. Written for medical students and residents; the 3D models are procedural and rotatable. For the receptor-by-receptor companion — every transmitter, receptor family, and transporter these cells express — see the Neurotransmitter & Receptor Atlas.

These models are generated procedurally in the browser rather than derived from imaging data. They are schematic teaching objects — proportions are chosen for legibility, not measured. For expert-segmented anatomical geometry, use the 3D Brain Explorer.

Systemic physiology is not a backdrop to neuronal function — it is a set of operating parameters. Protons gate the NMDA receptor. Magnesium plugs its pore. Sodium powers every monoamine transporter. Temperature sets channel kinetics. The tables below map each derangement to the specific molecule it acts on, then to the bedside.
