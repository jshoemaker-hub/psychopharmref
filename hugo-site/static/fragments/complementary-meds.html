Clinical decision-support only. Two engines: a curated knowledge base of established combination/augmentation strategies (each tagged by evidence tier — FDA-approved adjunct, guideline, or empirical), shown above a computed ranking derived from receptor-binding divergence (30%), action divergence at shared receptors (15%), signal-transduction tier divergence (15%), drug class (15%), and shared FDA indications (25%). Contraindicated pairs (e.g. MAOI plus a serotonergic agent) are never ranked; they are listed separately with the reason. Action data (full/partial agonist, antagonist, inverse agonist, reuptake inhibitor, releaser, allosteric modulator) is curated pharmacology, not a measured constant. Neither establishes the efficacy or safety of a combination for a given patient — always assess CYP450 interactions, additive serotonergic/QT/sedation/seizure risk, and whether optimizing monotherapy is preferable. Use alongside clinical judgment.

◎ Residual symptoms to target (optional)

Tick what the patient still has on the current agent. Candidates are then scored on whether their mechanism plausibly covers those domains — and flagged where it may worsen them. Leave blank to rank on mechanism alone.

◫ Matching weights

Adjust how much each factor counts. Raise Receptor divergence to favor agents hitting different targets; raise Action divergence to favor agents that act differently at the same target (e.g. a D2 antagonist paired with a D2 partial agonist); raise Cascade-tier divergence to favor agents working at a different level of signal transduction (lithium and valproate act intracellularly rather than at a receptor); lower Class to surface options in other classes. Axes that cannot be measured for a given pair are dropped and their weight redistributed — Symptom coverage only applies once you tick a residual symptom above. Uncheck “Restrict to same category” above to widen the pool. Weights normalize to 100%; results re-rank as you drag. (Eligible agents must still share ≥1 indication and diverge mechanistically.)
