Educational tool — not a diagnosis. The HITS is a screen, not a diagnostic instrument, and the danger review supports (does not replace) clinical judgment or a specialist assessment. Screening or asking about abuse in the presence of the partner can escalate danger — interview the patient alone, never use family or the partner as interpreter, and verify what is safe to document and to release before completing this form. A positive or high-risk result should prompt validation, warm referral to a DV advocate, the National DV Hotline (1-800-799-7233), and attention to your jurisdiction’s mandatory-reporting duties.
Interview the patient privately. Frame screening universally ("Because abuse is common and affects health, I ask everyone"). Complete Part A (HITS) with the patient; complete Part B (danger review) from the interview and record. The highest-risk period is during and just after leaving.

Interpretation & Response

HITS ≥ 11 (positive): discuss the result, validate ("this is not your fault"), assess danger, and make a warm referral. A negative screen does not rule out abuse — clinical suspicion overrides a negative screen.

Any high-risk marker (Part B): firearm access, prior strangulation, threats to kill, or the patient's belief the partner could kill them — prioritize urgent, private safety planning with a DV advocate and address medical needs (e.g., strangulation evaluation).

Response, in brief: believe and validate → respect autonomy (do not pressure to leave) → assess danger and children's safety → warm referral + hotline + safety plan → document objectively and safely → know your reporting duties.
