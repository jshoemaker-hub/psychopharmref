Clinical decision-support only. Similarity is computed from receptor-binding profiles, action match at shared receptors (both antagonists, both reuptake inhibitors, etc.), signal-transduction tier agreement, drug class, and shared FDA indications — and, when residual symptoms are ticked, how well each candidate’s mechanism still covers them. Any axis that cannot be measured for a pair is dropped and its weight redistributed. Pharmacologic similarity does not guarantee comparable efficacy or tolerability in an individual, and for apparent tachyphylaxis a switch to a different mechanism is sometimes preferable. Use alongside clinical judgment.

◎ Residual symptoms to keep covered (optional)

Tick what the patient still has. Candidates are then scored on whether their mechanism plausibly covers those domains — and flagged where it may worsen them — so a switch doesn’t trade one problem for another. Leave blank to rank on pharmacology alone.

◫ Matching weights

Adjust how much each factor counts toward the match. Raise Action match to favor agents that act the same way at shared receptors; raise Cascade-tier match to favor agents working at the same level of signal transduction. Lower Class to surface options in other classes; lower Shared indications to reach across indications. Axes that cannot be measured for a given pair are dropped and their weight redistributed — Symptom coverage only applies once you tick a residual symptom above. Uncheck “Restrict to same category” above to widen the pool. Weights normalize to 100%; results re-rank as you drag.
