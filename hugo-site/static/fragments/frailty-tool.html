Clinical & Documentation Use Only — SMI-FRAIL-20 is an educational documentation aid, not a validated psychometric instrument and not endorsed by CMS or any state Medicaid agency. It does not determine exemption eligibility; verification standards are set independently by each state. CMS's rule prohibits relying on diagnosis codes alone — pair every item scored 2–3 with a concrete, dated behavioral example in the chart note.
Policy context: CMS's June 2026 interim final rule requires states to verify not just a qualifying diagnosis but that the condition significantly impairs the patient's ability to meet the 80-hour/month Medicaid community engagement requirement. Describe one specific, dated, behavioral example — not a restated diagnosis. Cite your source (chart review, collateral, direct observation). Example: "Per residential staff report on 6/2/26, patient required prompting for hygiene on 4 of 7 days and missed a scheduled volunteer shift during a symptom exacerbation."
Under the CMS rule, the SUD frailty category applies whether or not the patient is currently in active treatment, but excludes individuals in stable, sustained recovery for five or more years.
SMI Frailty Score Interpretation (0–60)
0–10 Minimal Exemption unlikely to be supported on this domain alone; continue routine monitoring. 11–25 Mild-to-moderate May support a determination if impairment is concentrated in items 3, 4, 9, 18, 20. Narrative detail is essential. 26–40 Moderate-to-severe Generally supports a medical frailty determination. Document ADL/IADL, safety, and hospitalization findings. 41–60 Severe Strongly supports a medical frailty exemption. Document safety risk and inability to sustain 80 hrs/month of activity.
SUD Add-On Module Interpretation (0–24)
0–6 Low Low frailty contribution from SUD alone; combine with the SMI Frailty Score above. 7–14 Moderate Supports frailty determination when combined with SMI domains. 15–24 Severe Substance-related impairment may independently support a frailty determination.
These bands are a clinical organizing framework, not a CMS-published cutoff. States retain discretion over verification criteria — always pair the total score with item-level narrative.
