Home Safety Checklist

As judgment and memory change, everyday spaces carry new risks. A few adjustments keep your loved one safer and give you peace of mind.

🦶

Prevent Falls

  • Remove loose rugs, cords, and clutter from walkways.
  • Add grab bars in the bath and by the toilet.
  • Improve lighting; use night-lights on the path to the bathroom.
  • Non-slip mats; sturdy, closed shoes.
💊

Medications & Hazards

  • Lock up medications; use a pill organizer or locked dispenser.
  • Store cleaning products, alcohol, and tools out of reach.
  • Lower water heater to prevent scald burns.
  • Consider stove knob covers or an auto shut-off.
🚪

Wandering Prevention

  • Place locks high or low on exit doors, out of usual sight line.
  • Use door chimes or alarms; camera or motion alerts.
  • Have them wear an ID bracelet; consider GPS tracking.
  • Tell trusted neighbors, and keep a recent photo handy.
🚗

Driving

Driving often becomes unsafe as dementia progresses. Warning signs: getting lost, close calls, slow reactions, confusion at intersections.

Involve the clinician early — a formal driving evaluation or a doctor's recommendation can help make the conversation less personal.

📱

Emergency Ready

  • Post key phone numbers where they're easy to find.
  • Keep a list of medications and diagnoses by the door.
  • Working smoke and carbon-monoxide alarms.
  • Have a plan (and a backup person) if you're unavailable.
