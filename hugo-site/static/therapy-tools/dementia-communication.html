Therapy Tools · Dementia & Caregiving

Communicating & Daily Care

As words get harder for your loved one, how you say things matters more than what you say. Small changes in approach can turn a struggle into a calm moment.

💬

Set the Stage

  • Reduce noise — turn off the TV before talking.
  • Approach from the front; make gentle eye contact.
  • Use their name and identify yourself.
  • Get to their level and keep a calm, warm tone.
🗣

Keep Words Simple

  • Short sentences, one idea at a time.
  • Ask yes/no questions, not open-ended ones.
  • Offer two choices, not many: "Tea or juice?"
  • Give one step, wait, then the next.
⏳

Patience & Pauses

  • Allow extra time to process and respond — count to ten.
  • Don't interrupt or finish their sentences too fast.
  • If they can't find a word, offer it gently or move on.
  • Repeat the same way, using the same words.
❤

Validate, Don't Correct

Arguing with confusion rarely works and causes distress. Instead of correcting a false belief, respond to the feeling behind it.

If they ask for a parent who has died, try "You miss your mom — tell me about her" rather than "She passed years ago."

🥂

Everyday Care Tasks

  • Keep familiar routines and the same order each day.
  • Break bathing/dressing into small, announced steps.
  • Lay out clothing in the order it goes on.
  • Protect dignity — offer choices and cover for privacy.
🎭

Meaningful Activities

  • Favor familiar, simplified hobbies — folding, sorting, gardening.
  • Use music from their youth; it reaches when words don't.
  • Look at old photos and tell stories (reminiscence).
  • Focus on the process and connection, not the result.
Body language speaks loudest. A calm face, a soft voice, and an unhurried touch communicate safety even when words don't land. When a moment goes sideways, step back, reset your own tone, and try again later.
