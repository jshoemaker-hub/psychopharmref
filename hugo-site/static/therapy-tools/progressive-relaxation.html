Progressive Muscle Relaxation

Anxiety lives in the body as tension. By tensing and then releasing muscles in order, you teach your body the difference — and how to let go.

🧠

Why It Works

A tense body signals the brain that there's danger, which fuels more anxiety. Deliberately releasing muscle tension sends the opposite signal — safe, calm — and the nervous system follows.

With practice, you'll spot and drop tension earlier, before it builds.

⚙

How To Do It

  • Sit or lie somewhere comfortable, eyes closed.
  • Tense one muscle group 5 seconds (about 70%, not to pain).
  • Release all at once and notice the looseness for 10–15 seconds.
  • Breathe slowly throughout. Move to the next group.
🧍

The Sequence

  1. Hands & forearms — make fists
  2. Upper arms — bend elbows, tense
  3. Shoulders — shrug to ears
  4. Face — scrunch eyes, nose, jaw
  5. Chest & belly — tighten
  6. Legs & feet — tense, curl toes
🌿

Finish Well

  • When done, sit quietly and scan for any leftover tension — release it.
  • Notice how your body feels now versus when you started.
  • Take a few slow breaths before standing.
💡

Tips

  • Practice daily when calm — not just in crisis. Skill first, then use.
  • 10–15 minutes is ideal; a quick version (hands, shoulders, face) works in a pinch.
  • Skip tensing any injured or painful area — just relax it.
