What it measures

The Young Mania Rating Scale (YMRS; Young, Biggs, Ziegler and Meyer, 1978) is an 11-item clinician-administered scale for rating the severity of manic symptoms in adults. Ratings reflect the patient's behavior, speech and reported subjective experience during the interview. It is used for baseline assessment and for tracking treatment response in bipolar I and bipolar II disorder. It does not replace a full clinical evaluation.

Items and scoring

Seven items are scored 0–4: elevated mood, increased motor activity and energy, sexual interest, sleep, language and thought disorder, appearance, and insight. Four items carry double weight and are scored 0, 2, 4, 6 or 8: irritability, speech rate and amount, content, and disruptive or aggressive behavior. Every rating has its anchor description on screen. The total is the sum, from 0 to 60.

Interpreting the result

A YMRS of 12 or less is the conventional remission threshold used in most trials. The bands above 12 are descriptive, not validated severity categories. The tool notes that Lukasiewicz and colleagues (2013) mapped the YMRS to the CGI-BP with different breakpoints, finding a threshold of 25 for markedly ill mania in the EMBLEM cohort and a minimal clinically significant difference of 6.6 points. In the severe band the tool prompts assessment of safety, psychosis, impairment and the need for an urgent treatment setting. The severity label appears only after all 11 items are rated; before that the tool shows how many items are answered.

What the report includes

The copied plain-text report lists the date, total score out of 60, severity band, a clinical note for that band, each item score against its maximum, the scoring note on double-weighted items and band validity, and the Young 1978 citation. An on-screen grid shows item scores as you rate, and a Print Blank Form button produces a paper copy for bedside use.
