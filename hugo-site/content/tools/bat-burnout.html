BAT Burnout Assessment Tool: Scores and Cut-offs

Score the Burnout Assessment Tool (BAT-23 work or BAT-22 general) as 1–5 domain means, compare with the 2023 validated cut-offs, and copy a dated report.

What it measures

The Burnout Assessment Tool (BAT) was developed by Schaufeli, De Witte and Desart (2019) at KU Leuven. It is a self-report measure of burnout across four core dimensions: exhaustion, mental distance, cognitive impairment and emotional impairment. A 10-item secondary symptoms scale covers psychological complaints (sleep, worry, tension, anxiety, sensitivity to noise and crowds) and psychosomatic complaints (palpitations or chest pain, GI complaints, headaches, muscle pain, frequent illness). The BAT is a screening instrument, not a diagnostic tool.

Versions and scoring

Switch between the work-related version (23 core items: 8 exhaustion, 5 mental distance, 5 cognitive impairment, 5 emotional impairment) and the general version (22 core items, with 4 mental distance items). Each item is rated 1 (never) to 5 (always), with an N/A option. Scores are reported as means rather than sums: an overall core mean, a mean for each of the four dimensions, and a secondary symptoms mean. Items marked N/A are left out of the mean.

Interpreting the result

The cut-offs come from Schaufeli and colleagues (2023, Scandinavian Journal of Work, Environment and Health), using pooled Dutch, Flemish and Finnish samples. The tool also lists subscale cut-offs (at risk / severe): exhaustion 3.06 / 3.31, mental distance 2.10 / 3.30, cognitive impairment 2.70 / 3.10, and emotional impairment 2.30 / 2.90. These were derived for the work-related BAT-23 and should be applied with caution to the general version. Elevated scores warrant further clinical evaluation within a full assessment.
