What it measures

The Saint Louis University Mental Status Examination (SLUMS; Tariq et al., 2006) is a 30-point screening exam for mild neurocognitive disorder and dementia. The tool notes that its sensitivity to mild cognitive impairment may exceed that of the MMSE. It is a screening instrument and does not replace a full neuropsychological evaluation; results should be read alongside education, medical history, cultural factors and baseline function.

Items and scoring

The exam has 11 questions scored as 21 units. Orientation (day, year, state) earns 1 point each. Five words are registered in Q4 for later recall. The money problem scores 1 point for amount spent and 2 for amount left. Animal naming in one minute (with an on-screen timer) scores 0–3 points by count. Delayed recall of the five words earns 1 point each, digit span backwards 1 point per string, the clock 2 points for hour markers and 2 for the time, the shapes task 1 point each, and the story 2 points for each of four questions.

Domain analysis

The tool splits the total into seven domains: orientation (3), executive function (3, from the money problem), attention (2, from the shapes task), episodic memory (13, from word and story recall), working memory (2), language and processing speed (3, from animal fluency), and visuospatial construction (4, from the clock). Each domain shows a bar and, when points are lost, a short note on the brain regions involved and the clinical meaning. Memory carries the largest weight; the tool notes that disproportionate memory loss suggests an Alzheimer-pattern impairment.
