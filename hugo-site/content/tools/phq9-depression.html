PHQ-9 Depression Screener: Score and Severity Bands

Score the 9-item PHQ-9 (0–27) for depression screening and severity, with an Item 9 safety alert and a plain-text report for the chart.

Open the PHQ-9 Calculator

Runs free in your browser. Scores are calculated on your device and not sent to a server.

What it measures

The Patient Health Questionnaire-9 (PHQ-9) asks how often, over the last 2 weeks, the patient has been bothered by nine problems: loss of interest or pleasure, low mood, sleep change, fatigue, appetite change, feelings of failure or guilt, poor concentration, psychomotor slowing or restlessness, and thoughts of being better off dead or of self-harm. It is a screening and severity tool, not a standalone diagnostic instrument. A diagnosis still requires a clinical interview and judgment.

Items and scoring

Each item is rated 0 (Not at all), 1 (Several days), 2 (More than half the days) or 3 (Nearly every day). The total is the sum of the nine items, from 0 to 27. The calculator shows the running total and an answered count, and assigns a severity band only once all nine items are answered.

ScoreSeveritySuggested action shown by the tool
0–4None–MinimalNone indicated
5–9MildWatchful waiting; repeat PHQ-9 at follow-up
10–14ModerateTreatment plan, considering counseling, follow-up, and/or pharmacotherapy
15–19Moderately SevereActive treatment with pharmacotherapy and/or psychotherapy
20–27SevereImmediate pharmacotherapy and, if severe impairment or poor response to therapy, expedited referral to a mental health specialist

An optional final question records how difficult the problems have made work, home life and getting along with others, from "Not difficult at all" to "Extremely difficult". It does not change the score.

Item 9 safety alert

Any response above 0 on Item 9 (thoughts of being better off dead or of self-harm) displays an alert to conduct a direct suicidal ideation assessment and safety evaluation. The flag is also printed in the report. The alert is a prompt to ask further questions; it is not a risk assessment on its own.
