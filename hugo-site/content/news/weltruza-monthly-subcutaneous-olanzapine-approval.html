Key points

  • On October 9, 2026, Teva announced FDA approval of Weltruza (olanzapine extended-release injectable suspension), a once-monthly subcutaneous olanzapine for adults with schizophrenia. During development it was called TEV-'749.
  • Weltruza needs no loading dose, no oral overlap, no booster injections and no post-injection observation. Zyprexa Relprevv, the older intramuscular olanzapine pamoate, requires at least three hours of observation under a REMS.
  • The label carries the class boxed warning on mortality in older adults with dementia-related psychosis. It also states that it is not known whether post-injection delirium/sedation syndrome (PDSS) can occur.

For more than fifteen years, clinicians who wanted olanzapine in a long-acting form had one choice, and it came with a waiting room. Every injection of Zyprexa Relprevv (olanzapine pamoate) has to be followed by at least three hours of continuous observation in a certified setting. The reason is post-injection delirium/sedation syndrome, or PDSS. That requirement helps explain why olanzapine never became a widely used depot.

On October 9, 2026, Teva announced that the FDA had approved Weltruza (olanzapine extended-release injectable suspension). It is given once a month as a subcutaneous injection and has no observation requirement. The company said it expects US availability in the coming weeks. An application is also under review at the European Medicines Agency.

One product, several names

Weltruza is the olanzapine depot Teva and Medincell have presented at meetings for two years. Teva developed it as TEV-'749, submitted the NDA in December 2025, and the FDA accepted the application in February 2026. The drug uses Medincell's SteadyTeq copolymer technology. After subcutaneous injection, the polymer precipitates into a depot that releases olanzapine slowly. The same platform underlies Uzedy (subcutaneous risperidone), approved in 2023, so Weltruza is the second US approval from the Teva–Medincell partnership.

What SOLARIS showed

The approval rests on the phase 3 SOLARIS trial, which enrolled 675 adults aged 18 to 64 with schizophrenia. Its first period was an 8-week, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled comparison of three doses. An open-label safety extension of up to 48 weeks followed, giving up to 56 weeks of exposure in total.

  • Efficacy: All three doses met the primary endpoint, which was change in PANSS total score at week 8 compared with placebo. In Teva's 2024 topline release, the placebo-adjusted reductions were roughly 10 to 11 points (adjusted p < 0.001 for each dose). CGI-S and Personal and Social Performance (PSP) scores also separated from placebo after multiplicity adjustment.
  • PDSS: Teva reported no PDSS events through 56 weeks. At the time of the 2024 topline release, about 80% of the target number of injections had been given.
  • Local and metabolic effects: Coverage of the long-term data reported injection-site induration and pain in 12% of participants each, erythema in 10% and pruritus in 7%. Weight gain was reported in 36%, and the overall systemic profile was described as consistent with oral olanzapine.

Most of these figures come from company releases and trade coverage of meeting presentations, not from a full peer-reviewed paper. The extension data are open-label.

The Relprevv label puts the risk in context: PDSS occurred in under 0.1% of injections, which was about 2% of patients treated for up to 46 months. The episodes were serious, though. Symptoms ranged from confusion and slurred speech to coma, and many patients were hospitalized. Because of the REMS, the prescriber, the facility, the pharmacy and the patient all have to be enrolled.

The new label's wording on PDSS deserves attention. "Not known whether it can occur" means it has not been seen, not that it has been ruled out. A syndrome that appears in fewer than one in a thousand injections can easily go undetected in a single program of a few hundred patients.

Clinical angle

The clearest candidates are adults already stabilized on oral olanzapine at 10 to 20 mg/day who would benefit from monthly dosing but who were never offered Relprevv because of the three-hour observation period. That group includes patients with partial adherence, frequent relapses or long travel to clinic. The labeled dose equivalence makes the switch easy to calculate. Starting an LAI does not change the usual olanzapine safety work. Baseline and follow-up weight, glucose and lipids still apply, and the 36% weight-gain figure supports building metabolic monitoring into the injection schedule. Check the full prescribing information for initiation in patients not already established on olanzapine, for older adults and for hepatic considerations. Teach patients and staff the signs of excessive sedation or confusion after a dose.

What we still don't know

As of this writing we could not find a published list price or a date for first shipments. There are no head-to-head data against other monthly LAIs. Real-world PDSS surveillance will only begin once the drug is in wide use. Postmarketing experience will show whether the convenience holds up.
