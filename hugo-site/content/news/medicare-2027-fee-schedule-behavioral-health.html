Key points

  • CMS proposed the CY 2027 Medicare physician fee schedule on July 14, 2026. As of November 3, when this piece was written, the final rule had not been posted. Whatever it says takes effect January 1, 2027.
  • The proposal lowers the conversion factor by about 1.2% to 1.7%, mostly because a one-year 2.5% raise that Congress gave for 2026 expires. Psychiatric collaborative care, SBIRT and the last step of a psychotherapy increase would partly offset that.
  • The tele-mental health rules don't depend on this rule. Under the Consolidated Appropriations Act, 2026, the in-person visit requirement for Medicare mental health telehealth is delayed to January 1, 2028, and audio-only visits stay allowed until then.

Each fall CMS finalizes the physician fee schedule that sets Medicare payment for the coming year. Commercial payers often use it as a benchmark too. Last year's final rule went on public display on October 31. This year's (CMS-1848-F) is expected in November, but it had not been posted when this piece was written. Comments on the proposal closed September 14. Below is what CMS proposed for behavioral health and what is already settled by statute. Some numbers may change in the final rule.

A smaller conversion factor

CMS estimated two 2027 conversion factors. For clinicians who qualify through an advanced alternative payment model (APM), it is $33.1693, about 1.19% lower than this year. For everyone else, it is $32.8409, about 1.68% lower. Most psychiatrists bill under the second one. The main cause is the expiration of the 2.5% statutory increase that applied only to services furnished in 2026. A 0.53% budget-neutrality adjustment and the statutory 0.75% (qualifying APM) or 0.25% (non-qualifying) updates only partly offset that loss. Code-level changes elsewhere in the rule will move individual payments up or down from there.

Collaborative care gets a significant increase

Primary care practices and psychiatric consultants have said for years that the Psychiatric Collaborative Care Model (CoCM) codes were undervalued. CMS proposed revaluing the codes using a blend of level 3 and level 4 office visits. Advocates had asked for a level 4 and 5 blend, which CMS declined, citing how rarely level 5 visits are billed.

The add-on CoCM codes billed with advanced primary care management (G0568, G0569) would follow the same values. CMS also proposed raising the per-minute labor rate for the behavioral health care manager from $0.57 to $0.70. It would do this by pegging the role to a social worker/psychologist rate rather than to genetic counselors, the comparison used when the codes were created. The same rate increase would also apply to the monthly office-based opioid use disorder bundles, G2086 through G2088.

Psychotherapy, SBIRT and tobacco cessation

2027 is the fourth and final year of the 19.1% increase to time-based psychotherapy work values that CMS finalized in the CY 2024 rule. CMS proposed giving the full 19.1% at once to services last valued in 2008 or 2019. These are screening, brief intervention and referral to treatment (SBIRT: G0396 from 0.65 to 0.77, G0397 from 1.30 to 1.55, G2011 from 0.33 to 0.39) and tobacco cessation counseling (99406 from 0.24 to 0.29, 99407 from 0.50 to 0.60).

Telehealth: settled by Congress through 2027

Section 6209 of the Consolidated Appropriations Act, 2026 extended the main Medicare telehealth flexibilities. It waives the geographic and originating-site limits through December 31, 2027. It delays the requirement for an in-person visit within six months before tele-mental health care, and every 12 months during it, until January 1, 2028. It also keeps audio-only telehealth until that date. The same in-person waiver applies to mental health visits at rural health clinics and FQHCs through December 31, 2027. The fee schedule proposal only makes conforming changes to the regulations. One new item for 2027 is that the law requires new claim modifiers for telehealth delivered through a virtual platform the clinician contracts with or pays to use. The modifiers do not change payment.

The proposed rule also creates codes for shared medical appointments: 60-minute group sessions of up to 10 established patients, in person or by telehealth. These are limited to conditions that respond to lifestyle change, such as obesity and diabetes, so they are not a new code for group psychotherapy. The proposal does not address digital mental health treatment device codes.

Clinical angle

If the collaborative care values are finalized, CoCM becomes one of the better-paid ways for a psychiatrist to cover a large primary care population. Practices that dropped CoCM because it lost money may want to rerun the numbers. For tele-psychiatry, the Medicare in-person visit requirement does not apply until January 1, 2028. That is a different rule from the DEA's controlled-substance prescribing deadline of December 31, 2026, so keep the two dates separate in patient planning.

We will update this story when CMS releases the final rule.
