# Reference audit, October 2026

This note covers three reviews done together: numbered reference lists in the chapters, chapters that had no reference list, medication-card monitoring schedules, and the Zyprexa Relprevv REMS update.

Journal articles were checked against PubMed (E-utilities esearch/esummary) and Crossref. A citation was kept when the record matched the authors and the title. Metadata that was wrong on a real paper (year, volume, issue, pages, journal abbreviation, DOI) was rewritten from the API record. Citations with no matching paper, or a real paper that does not support the chapter, were removed and the list was renumbered. Books, statutes, DSM and ICD entries, and agency reports that are not journal articles were left in place when they could not be confirmed as fabricated.

## Totals

- Chapters with a numbered reference list that was audited: 96
- Citations kept as verified: 672
- Citations kept as non-journal sources (books, guidelines, statutes, datasets): 319
- Citations corrected from the API record: 69 (4 metadata-only, 65 author or locator rewrite of the same title)
- Citations removed: 565
- Ten of those removals were real papers whose journal name did not share a token with the citation (for example Arch Fam Med versus Arch Intern Med). Those ten were put back, using the PubMed record, at the end of the reference list.
- One candidate restore was not put back: a bright-light trial whose verified title is nonseasonal major depression, which does not belong in the seasonal affective disorder chapter.

Paragraph bibliographies (unnumbered, split on a middot) were classified but not rewritten automatically:

- `antipsychiatry-rosenhan`: kept 3, non-journal 5, would remove 0

## Zyprexa Relprevv REMS

The REMS was not released. FDA approved supplements S-040 (CBE-30, received December 17, 2021) and S-043 (PAS, received April 3, 2024) to NDA 022173 on May 7, 2026. The electronic signature on the approval letter is Tiffany R. Farchione, May 7, 2026, 06:17:48 PM. The FDA safety labeling changes index lists May 7, 2026 (SUPPL-43). The label was revised May 2026, and the REMS document says "Most Recent REMS Update: 05/2026."

What changed in that approval: removal of "single-use" wording; the program name is Zyprexa Relprevv REMS; the goal is safe use with respect to post-injection delirium/sedation syndrome; materials were aligned to the January 2023 REMS guidance; prescribers and healthcare settings have knowledge assessments at certification and recertification; pharmacies must obtain authorization to dispense before each dispense; audit requirements include wholesaler-distributors; the REMS website includes certified pharmacy and healthcare-setting finders.

What remains required: at least 3 hours of continuous observation in a certified healthcare setting that can respond to an emergency; the patient must be alert, oriented, and free of post-injection delirium/sedation syndrome before leaving; the patient must be accompanied after leaving; the prescriber, healthcare setting, patient, and pharmacy must enroll; the product must not be dispensed directly to a patient. Enrollment phone: 1-877-772-9390.

## Medication-card monitoring

Card data lives in `js/data.js` (`MEDICATIONS`). No medication had a structured monitoring schedule on the card. Schedules were added in `js/monitoring-data.js` and copied onto each medication as `monitoring`. The drug modal and the BHI reference card show the full schedule. The hover card shows a one-line summary.

Antipsychotic metabolic timing follows the ADA, APA, AACE, and NAASO consensus (Diabetes Care. 2004;27(2):596-601): weight at baseline, 4, 8, and 12 weeks, then quarterly; waist at baseline and yearly; blood pressure, fasting glucose, and fasting lipids at baseline and 12 weeks, then yearly (lipids every 5 years if normal). Drug-specific labs follow the current prescribing information: clozapine ANC (weekly for 6 months, every 2 weeks for 6 months, then monthly; REMS eliminated in 2025, the lab schedule remains), lithium (12-hour level, renal function, TSH, calcium), valproate (liver tests and platelets), carbamazepine (CBC, liver tests, HLA-B*1502), oxcarbazepine (sodium), topiramate (bicarbonate), and the labeled vital-sign checks for stimulants, SNRIs, esketamine, and buprenorphine.

Where the label does not require standing bloodwork, the card says "No routine lab monitoring required" and lists the clinical checks (suicidality, blood pressure, rash, sedation, and so on).
